# AI Short Drama

Turn a single payoff motif into an AI short-drama system that can be tested, serialized, keeps characters from drifting, and gives viewers a reason to watch the next episode every time. Story and serial state are owned by this Skill; image, video, and voice models are only downstream executors.

When the user asks for full automation ("from topic to finished film"), an Auto mode runs a dependency gate and advances a resumable project on its own, stopping only at sign-in / payment / capability / compliance blocks and final public release — and never claiming a finished film exists when the assembly executor is missing.

## 1. Boundaries and cross-Skill routing

This Skill owns genre, concept, character relationships, season arcs, episode hooks, and foreshadowing / payoff debt. Character and scene images, video prompts, voices, model selection, film assembly, and Shorts/Reels packaging are routed to downstream media, editing, and packaging skills through a shared production contract.

## 2. Eight Modes

Eight modes cover the full lifecycle: Scout (opportunity research), Greenlight (concept scoring), Bible (world and characters), Season (multi-episode planning), Episode (a shootable script plus state delta), Studio (format routing and shot timelines), Produce (the production pack), and Audit (layered diagnosis).

## 3. Default workflow

The workflow runs from defining the distribution surface and choosing a "validated engine × fresh variable," through the Greenlight Score and building the Series Bible, to making a 3–5 episode pilot first, then compiling single episodes, the Studio Plan, and finally the Production Pack — each validated by the included lint scripts before generation tasks are compiled.

## 4. Hard rules for satisfying drama

Ten hard rules keep the drama satisfying: every humiliation opens a payoff debt, only one layer of cards is revealed at a time, antagonists must learn, systems have costs and blind spots, and a micro-arc completes every 3–5 episodes.

## 5. Hard rules for AI production

Production rules lock characters, scenes, props, costumes, and voices to fixed IDs, lock stills before image-to-video, track `state_before → event → state_after` for every episode, and require a verified model-capability snapshot before each production run.

## 6. Audit and learning

Problems are diagnosed by layer — concept, episode, series, production, and packaging — rather than blaming every retention drop on prompts. Only one main variable is tested at a time, and a pattern becomes a default only when at least three works point the same way.

## 7. Delivery format

By default the delivery is a one-sentence recommendation, the Premise Contract, protagonist / antagonist / system cards, the reveal, antagonist, and payoff ladders, a 3-episode pilot table, a shootable Episode 1 script, and summaries of the Studio Plan and Production Pack, plus risks and a validation plan.
