# Character consistency

The core principle: separate every character definition into locked identity anchors and narrative variables that may change by shot — and never solve a narrative change by silently changing a locked identity anchor.

## Lock the identity first

Lock face shape, jawline, eye shape and spacing, nose and mouth proportions, age range, skin tone, hairline, core hairstyle silhouette, signature features, body proportion and identity-critical marks; let expression, pose, wardrobe, camera, scene and lighting vary. Mark each attribute locked, preferred or variable; fill a brief (name, age, build, face, hair, outfit item by item, close-up, style) and show it back before writing prompts.

## Build the close-up and the sheet

Expand every brief field into specifics — vague prompts produce generic faces, and a generic face cannot stay consistent. Write the close-up prompt eyes-first, generate candidates, and pick one the user would recognize in a crowd. Then build one three-panel sheet (bust-up front, full body front, full body rear) on a seamless cool grey background, restating the full identity and outfit; make a second sheet for wardrobe changes.

## Carry the reference into every shot

Attach the picked close-up or sheet as the identity reference on every generation where the character appears, with a role line stating it defines the facial features, hairstyle and clothing (not the grey background or panel layout). Order each shot prompt identity-first; describe motion separately from identity in video prompts.

## Check drift and fix at the cheapest level

Compare each result to the canonical reference, checking facial geometry and identity anchors before clothing, pose and style; classify drift as none, minor, material or redesign — never inventing a numeric similarity score. Fix small drift with a one-line corrective guideline before rebuilding the sheet or regenerating the shot, and deliver the full prompt package (brief, prompts, role line, continuity constraints).

## Alternatives for better results

Route repair of existing footage to a video face-continuity workflow when only identity is wrong, and a recurring character problem to a heavier style-consistency method when a corrective guideline and a sheet have both failed.
